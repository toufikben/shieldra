# Shieldra Phase 4 Decision Register — Legacy Technical Register

**Status:** Superseded as the primary authority by [`../PHASE4_DECISION_REGISTER.md`](../PHASE4_DECISION_REGISTER.md) and [`../../SHIELDRA_DECISIONS.md`](../../SHIELDRA_DECISIONS.md). Retained as a detailed technical analysis and open-question record.
**Current interpretation:** G0 is approved for internal development; approved G1 Guard rules may proceed into pure contract closure. Platform, evidence, storage implementation, cryptography, monitoring, and provider details remain controlled or blocked until their specific decisions are closed.
**Purpose:** Separate documented facts from Android constraints, engineering decisions, assumptions, and unknowns without silently selecting unresolved behavior.

## Classification rules

- **FACT:** explicitly established by repository documentation.
- **ANDROID CONSTRAINT:** platform behavior that implementation must respect.
- **ENGINEERING DECISION:** a choice required before implementation.
- **ASSUMPTION:** not established and therefore unsafe to use as a requirement.
- **UNKNOWN:** information still required.

## Decision register

| Area | Decision required | Possible choices and consequences | Android constraint | Security/privacy consequence | Cannot safely assume | Exact information required |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Authority | Approve Phase 4 scope | Use an official Phase 4 specification; or create an explicitly approved engineering decision package. Without either, runtime behavior remains blocked. | Android APIs cannot determine product intent. | Unapproved security behavior can create false protection claims. | That the Phase 3 UI implies requirements. | Product Freeze v5, Phase 1 Sections 1–43, official Phase 4 specification, and external-review authorization. |
| Lock Guard | Define trigger and observation model | Device-admin/enterprise observation, a supported platform signal, or a deliberately unsupported/deferred feature. Each choice has different feasibility and coverage. | Generic apps do not receive all failed-unlock details universally. | False positives or missed attacks affect user safety. | That failed unlocks can be observed on every Android device. | Supported API/OEM matrix, trigger, threshold, corroboration, debounce, cooldown, cancellation, repetition, severity, lifecycle, and failure UX. |
| Motion Guard | Define motion algorithm | Accelerometer/gyroscope thresholds, windowed classifier, or deferred feature. More sensitivity increases false positives and battery use. | Sensor availability, sampling, Doze, process death, and OEM limits apply. | Motion telemetry may reveal sensitive behavior. | Any default threshold or sensitivity. | Trigger, sensor set, sampling/window, calibration, debounce, battery budget, supported lifecycle, data minimization, and test device matrix. |
| SIM Guard | Define change semantics | Subscription identity, carrier/eSIM changes, or deferred feature. Identifiers may be unavailable or ambiguous. | Multi-SIM/eSIM and restricted identifiers vary by API and device. | Telephony metadata is sensitive. | That a carrier change equals tampering. | Baseline fields, supported devices, permissions, privacy classification, change rules, and recovery behavior. |
| Battery Guard | Define emergency threshold | Fixed threshold, hysteresis bands, charger exemptions, or deferred feature. Threshold changes urgency and battery impact. | Shutdown races and OEM battery policy can prevent work. | Battery state may be low-sensitivity but can reveal routines. | A threshold such as 15% or 20%. | Threshold, hysteresis, charging rules, event severity, delivery fallback, and offline behavior. |
| Panic | Define emergency semantics | Tap, long press, confirmation, authentication override, local feedback, or cancellation. Each affects accidental activation and safety. | Background and notification behavior are limited by Android. | A false belief that emergency services were contacted is high risk. | That Panic means SMS, a call, or emergency-service contact. | Exact trigger, confirmation, cancellation, authentication, cooldown, recipient/provider, failure disclosure, and liability wording. |
| Event confirmation | Define signal-to-event rules | Single signal, corroboration, weighted rules, or explicit user confirmation. More corroboration reduces false positives but may miss events. | Android only supplies observations; it does not define security severity. | Incorrect events can expose evidence or alert recipients. | That every signal creates a confirmed event. | Signal strengths, combinations, temporal windows, precedence, deduplication, cooldown, severity, and user-visible state. |
| Lifecycle | Resolve state model | Retain Group C seven-state model; add `EXPIRED`; or define a separate retention state. Each affects retries and UI. | Persistence and process death require durable state semantics. | Expired evidence and delivery records may still be sensitive. | That `EXPIRED` can be added without migration and UI impact. | Authoritative state list, terminality, expiry timing, retry/resume behavior, migration, and UI representation. |
| EventPipeline | Define ownership and operations | Pure coordinator over adapters, repository transaction workflow, or another approved design. More orchestration requires stronger recovery guarantees. | Background execution and process death affect stage completion. | Partial evidence/delivery must not be presented as complete. | That marker interfaces authorize an implementation API. | Operations, stage ownership, result/error types, ordering, cancellation, persistence boundaries, idempotency, concurrency, and recovery. |
| Camera evidence | Define capture policy | User-initiated, automatic on confirmed event, or no camera. Automatic capture increases privacy and permission risk. | Camera permission and lifecycle rules apply; background capture is restricted. | Photos are highly sensitive and can expose bystanders. | That a Photo chip means a photo was captured. | Trigger, preview/UX, consent, permission denial, storage, validation, retention, deletion, export, backup, and redaction. |
| Location evidence | Define location policy | Current location only, last-known fallback, coarse/fine location, or no location. Accuracy and freshness affect usefulness. | Background location requires additional permission and user disclosure. | Coordinates can reveal home, work, and routines. | That `LAST_KNOWN` is `CURRENT`, or that any coordinate is sufficiently accurate. | Precision, age/accuracy thresholds, source priority, consent, offline behavior, retention, deletion, and presentation labels. |
| Encryption | Choose cryptographic architecture | Encrypted files plus Keystore, encrypted database, or another reviewed design. Convenience affects migration and recovery. | Keystore keys can be invalidated and may be authentication-bound. | Incorrect key lifecycle can expose or permanently lose evidence. | Algorithm, key size, key recovery, or backup behavior. | Approved algorithms/libraries, key lifecycle, rotation, invalidation, recovery, versioning, backup exclusion, and threat-model sign-off. |
| Authentication | Choose identity and step-up model | Device credential/biometric, app PIN, external identity provider, or anonymous mode. Each changes recovery and threat surface. | Biometric/device credential APIs have enrollment and invalidation behavior. | Sensitive settings, evidence, and delivery configuration require enforcement. | That `AuthenticationGate` alone provides authentication. | Authenticator, session lifecycle, lockout, recovery, reauthentication duration, `SensitiveOperation` mapping, secure storage, and failure UX. |
| Storage | Choose local technology | DataStore for preferences, Room for structured data, encrypted database, or another reviewed option. | Migration, corruption, backup, and process death must be handled. | Plaintext credentials/evidence/settings are unsafe. | That a Room boundary authorizes a schema, or that DemoData is a schema. | Typed settings/data model, defaults, sensitivity, technology, schema, migration, corruption, deletion, restore, backup, and authentication protection. |
| Background execution | Choose lifecycle strategy | Foreground service, WorkManager, receiver, or user-initiated work. Reliability and battery impact differ. | Doze, OEM restrictions, quotas, and foreground-service type/permission rules apply. | Missed detection or duplicate processing affects safety and privacy. | That background monitoring is continuous or guaranteed. | Supported Android versions, lifecycle, boot/restart, Doze policy, battery budget, notification requirement, and recovery semantics. |
| Notifications | Define user-visible notification behavior | Local notification, notification plus provider delivery, or no notification. | Notification permission and channel settings affect delivery. | Notification text may expose sensitive facts on lock screen. | That a delivery receipt means a notification was shown. | Permission, channel, lock-screen redaction, payload, timing, failure state, and accessibility copy. |
| Delivery | Choose providers and payload | Email, approved messaging provider, SMS, or no external delivery. Provider terms, cost, and availability differ. | Network, background work, rate limits, and provider callbacks are not guaranteed. | Recipient exposure and external retention require consent and minimization. | That WhatsApp/Telegram/SMS are interchangeable or available without credentials. | Provider, account ownership, sender/recipient model, templates, payload/evidence policy, consent, sandbox credentials, quotas, callbacks, and operations owner. |
| Queue/retry | Define idempotency and retry policy | Bounded retry with backoff, manual retry, dead-letter, or no retry. | Process death and connectivity changes can interrupt work. | Duplicates can spam recipients or leak evidence repeatedly. | Retry count, backoff, or `SUCCESS` without provider proof. | Idempotency key, attempt model, retryable errors, backoff/jitter, terminal failures, expiry, ordering, leases, recovery, and receipt proof. |
| Premium/billing | Choose purchase and entitlement source | Google Play Billing or another approved provider. Local-only flags are easy to tamper with. | Purchase states include pending, acknowledged, refunded, and revoked. | False entitlements and unverified purchases create financial/security risk. | Demo price `9.99` or a Buy button as product authority. | Provider, SKU, price/currency, verification, restore, refunds, revocation, offline behavior, account binding, and source of truth. |
| Ads | Define advertising policy | No ads, approved SDK, or mediation. Consent and entitlement suppression are required. | Regional consent and SDK lifecycle apply. | Tracking and personalized ads can create privacy exposure. | That an `Ad` placeholder authorizes an SDK. | Provider, formats, placements, consent, regional/child policy, identifiers, personalization, frequency caps, and Premium suppression. |
| Accounts/cloud | Define account and data lifecycle | Anonymous device-only, required account, or cloud account. Sync changes deletion and recovery requirements. | Reinstall/device replacement changes local state. | Cloud sync expands attack and privacy surface. | That Settings or Premium implies an account. | Account strategy, provider, sessions, multi-device behavior, sync, deletion/export, retention conflicts, region, backend, and support owner. |
| Release/security review | Define final acceptance | Internal review, independent security/privacy review, staged pilot, or release hold. | Device/OEM behavior cannot be proven by JVM tests. | Security claims require evidence beyond compilation. | That CI build success proves runtime protection. | Threat model, abuse cases, offline behavior, compromised-device assumptions, logging policy, data minimization, device matrix, rollback, kill switch, and sign-off. |
